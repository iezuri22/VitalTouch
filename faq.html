Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about our home care services.

General Questions

We provide non-medical home care services including homemaking (cleaning, laundry, meal prep), companionship, transportation, personal care assistance, and specialized Alzheimer's and dementia care. We have both Homemakers and Certified Nursing Assistants (CNAs) available depending on what kind of help is needed.

No, VitalTouch provides non-medical home care. Our caregivers assist with daily living activities, companionship, and personal care, but we do not provide skilled nursing services, administer medications, or perform medical procedures. We can, however, provide medication reminders.

We serve Chicago and throughout Cook County, including the South Side, West Side, North Side, and suburbs like Homewood, Harvey, Dolton, Calumet City, Oak Lawn, and many more. View our full service area list.

Yes — VitalTouch Health Network is licensed by the State of Illinois to provide home care, and we meet all state requirements to do so. Our caregivers are background-checked, insured, and bonded.

About Our Caregivers

Our caregivers include both trained Homemakers and Certified Nursing Assistants (CNAs). All caregivers are background-checked, trained in their respective roles, and many have specialized training in Alzheimer's and dementia care.

Yes, our caregivers are trained to work with clients experiencing Alzheimer's and dementia. They provide patient, understanding support including daily routine assistance, memory-enhancing activities, redirection techniques, and safety supervision.

We strive for continuity of care by assigning a primary caregiver whenever possible. This allows clients to build rapport and feel comfortable with a familiar face. If a backup caregiver is ever needed, we ensure they're fully briefed on the care plan.

We carefully match clients with caregivers based on care needs, personality compatibility, skills, and experience. We take time to understand each client's own preferences and requirements to find the best fit.

Original Medicare — the red, white and blue card on its own — covers short stretches of skilled care after a hospital stay, not the ongoing everyday help most families are looking for. We'd rather tell you that plainly than let you find out three phone calls later.

A Medicare Advantage plan is a different story. Many plans here now include a set number of in-home support hours each year as an extra benefit. It isn't printed on the card and it's rarely mentioned at enrollment. Tell us the plan name and we'll find out what it includes. More on how that works.

Often, yes. The VA has a Homemaker and Home Health Aide program that pays an agency for exactly this kind of everyday help at home, plus Veteran-Directed Care, where the veteran holds a budget and chooses who provides it. The way in is usually a VA social worker — ask for a referral to the Homemaker and Home Health Aide program.

There's also Aid and Attendance, an added monthly amount on a VA pension for veterans and surviving spouses who need help with daily living. A lot of families who qualify have simply never been told about it, and nobody should ever charge you a fee to file the claim. More on VA benefits.

Yes, and it's one of the most common conversations we have. Most long-term care policies cover care at home, not just nursing homes — and most families never file, because the paperwork is off-putting and nobody explained how to start.

Bring the policy to a free consultation. We'll read it with you, tell you plainly what it covers, and handle the visit documentation the insurer asks for. What to look for in the policy.

If a policy or a plan benefit is paying, what it costs you depends on what that covers — and checking is free. If you're paying on your own, rates depend on the level of care and the hours needed. Contact us for a free consultation and a personalized quote; there's no obligation.

Support for Adults Living With a Disability

No. We support adults of any age living with a disability — people in their thirties, forties, and fifties as well as their eighties. The help is the same kind of help; what changes is that you're likely working, running a household, or raising children alongside it, and you want support with specific parts of the day rather than with life in general. Here's how that works.

Yes — and that's the most frustrating gap in the whole system, because the answer exists and almost nobody mentions it. Illinois runs the Home Services Program, which funds help at home for adults under 60 living with a disability, specifically so people can stay in their own place instead of moving into a facility. It sits outside the senior system entirely, which is exactly why you keep being told you don't qualify for the programs people name first.

Call us and say you're asking about the Home Services Program. We'll walk you through how it works and point you to the right office. More detail here.

Yes. Hands-on help getting in and out of bed, a chair, or the shower is core to what our personal care staff do, and they're trained to do it safely — for their sake as much as yours. Transfers done by an untrained helper are the single most common way both people end up hurt.

What we don't do is skilled nursing: wound care, injections, catheter changes, ventilator management. If part of what you need is nursing, we'll say so on the first call rather than the third week, and point you toward who does it. Quite often the answer is both, working alongside each other.
