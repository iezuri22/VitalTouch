Six ways to pay for home care

Most families think it's Medicaid or paying everything yourself. There are more options. Call (708) 726-4536 and we'll check what you're covered for, free.

The six routes

  1. Medicaid
  2. The Illinois Home Services Program, for adults under 60 living with a disability
  3. Extra benefits inside a Medicare Advantage plan
  4. VA benefits, for anyone who served
  5. A long-term care insurance policy
  6. Paying on your own
If you qualify on income

Medicaid

Illinois Medicaid covers help at home (personal care, homemaking, companionship) for people who qualify on income and need, usually at no cost. Hours are set by a care assessment.

We can't bill Medicaid yet. We can tell you whether you're likely to qualify and what applying involves. If you need care before we can bill it, we'll point you to an agency that can.

Next step

Call us and we'll tell you where you likely stand.

For adults under 60 living with a disability

The Illinois Home Services Program

Illinois funds help at home (personal care, homemaking) for adults under 60 living with a disability, so they can stay out of a facility. It is separate from senior programs, with its own rules and application. It often works alongside Social Security disability benefits.

We aren't approved to be paid through this program yet. We're working on it. Apply anyway if it fits, because approval takes time.

Next step

Call and ask about the Home Services Program. We'll explain it and point you to the right office, whether or not you use us.

For anyone on a Medicare Advantage plan

Medicare Advantage extra benefits

Many Medicare Advantage plans include a set number of in-home support hours a year (meals, housekeeping, personal care, rides). It's rarely mentioned at enrollment.

  • Check the benefits booklet for in-home support, personal care, homemaker, or caregiver support
  • Some plans call it a flex allowance or supplemental benefit card
  • Hours usually reset each plan year; unused hours are lost
  • Benefits vary by plan, even between plans from the same insurer
Next step

Tell us the plan name and we'll find out what it includes.

For veterans and surviving spouses

VA benefits

The VA's Homemaker and Home Health Aide program pays an agency for everyday help at home. Veteran-Directed Care gives the veteran a budget to choose their own caregiver. Aid and Attendance adds a monthly amount to a VA pension for veterans and surviving spouses who need daily help.

  • Start with a VA social worker and ask for a Homemaker and Home Health Aide referral
  • Surviving spouses may qualify for Aid and Attendance
  • Separate from Medicare and Medicaid
  • No one should charge you a fee to file a VA claim
Next step

Ask the veteran's VA social worker, or call us and we'll help you find who to ask.

If you have a policy

Long-term care insurance

Most long-term care policies cover care at home, not just nursing homes. Many families never file because the paperwork looks hard.

  • The daily or monthly benefit amount (the most it pays)
  • The elimination period (waiting days before benefits start)
  • Whether it requires a licensed agency (we are one)
  • Whether it needs an assessment first, and who can do it
Next step

Bring the policy to a free consultation. We'll read it with you and handle the visit records the insurer asks for.

For everyone, or while you wait on a program

Paying on your own

The most flexible option. You choose the hours and change them anytime. It also covers the gap while a program application is pending.

  • No minimums, no long contracts, month to month
  • Start small and add hours as needed
  • Monthly care plans if you want a set schedule and price
Next step

See monthly care plans or our rates, then call for a quote.

What Original Medicare covers

Original Medicare does not pay for ongoing everyday help at home. It covers short periods of skilled care after a hospital stay. If that's all you have, one of the other five routes is usually the answer. Call (708) 726-4536.
