Training
New hire must complete 24-hour pre-hire training
After onboarding paperwork is done, every new homemaker must complete 24 hours of training before their first client assignment. Training happens in two phases: "Prior to Assignment" and "Within 30 Days."
▼
Phase 1 — Before first assignment
A.2 — Initial 5-Hour Training (Prior) Open
20-section online training covering basic home care skills, safety, infection control, and client rights. Employee self-paces through all sections.
Employee completes training Auto-tracked by system
Immediately after A.2
B.2 — Competency Eval: 5 Hr (Prior) Open
22-question multiple choice exam. Must score 80% (18/22) to pass. If they fail, they retake the training and re-test.
Employee takes exam Auto-scored by system
After passing B.2
E.14 — 24-Hour Pre-Hire Training Ack Open
Employee acknowledges they understand the training requirements and their obligation to complete all 24 hours. This is the gatekeeper form — sign this, and you can be assigned clients.
Employee signs CEO countersigns
Phase 2 — Within 30 days of hire
A.3 — Initial 5-Hour Training (30 Days) Open
25-section advanced training covering nutrition, emergency response, documentation, and specialized care topics.
Employee completes training Auto-tracked by system
Immediately after A.3
B.3 — Competency Eval: 5 Hr (30 Days) Open
22-question exam on advanced topics. Must score 80% (18/22) to pass. Failure means repeat training and retest.
Employee takes exam Auto-scored by system
🚨 Hard deadline: Phase 2 training (A.3 + B.3) must be completed within 30 calendar days of hire. Missing this deadline is a compliance violation under 77 Ill. Adm. Code 245.71. Track every new hire's 30-day window.

▼
Immediately — within the hour
C.44 — Incident Report Form Open
Document everything: what happened, when, where in the home, who was present, what the homemaker did, client's condition after the fall. Include whether 911 was called and family was notified.
Homemaker fills out initial report Supervisor reviews & completes
Within 24 hours
C.22 — Home Risk Assessment (update) Open
Re-assess the home for the fall hazard that caused the incident. Document what changed and what corrective measures were recommended (grab bars, mats, furniture rearrangement).
Supervisor conducts & signs Client acknowledges & signs
Within 48 hours — if needed
C.24 — Plan of Care (update) Open
If the fall reveals new mobility limitations or if the doctor orders changes, update the Plan of Care with new safety instructions, increased supervision, or modified services.
CEO reviews & signs Client signs updated plan
🚨 IDPH reporting: If the fall results in hospitalization, broken bones, or is suspected to involve neglect, IDPH must be notified. Incident report must be filed within 24 hours — no exceptions.
📞 Notify: Client's family, client's physician, and the referring CCU (if CCP client). Document who you notified and when in the incident report.

Applicant's background check comes back with a disqualifying finding
You've been onboarding a promising applicant. Three days in, the IDPH background check results arrive and there's a hit — either a disqualifying conviction, a sex offender registry match, or an abuse/neglect finding on the HCWR. Now what?
▼
Immediately — upon receiving results
E.25 — IDPH Registry Uploads Open
Upload the background check results to the employee file even though the person won't be hired. The results and your response must be documented. This is your proof of due diligence if IDPH ever audits the decision.
Admin uploads results CEO reviews finding
Same day
No form — adverse action notification
Notify the applicant that their background check returned a disqualifying finding and VitalTouch cannot proceed with employment. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), you must provide: the name of the background check company, a copy of the report, and a notice of their right to dispute the finding.
CEO makes the call or sends the letter
Same day — stop all onboarding
No form — operational halt
Immediately revoke any system access. If the applicant was given login credentials to CareSmartz360 or any training platform during onboarding, disable them. Do not proceed with any training or orientation activities. The applicant must not have access to any client information.
Admin revokes access
🚨 77 Ill. Adm. Code 955.115: Disqualifying offenses include any felony conviction, sex offenses, crimes of violence, theft from a person with a disability, and any finding on the IDPH Nurse Aide Registry for abuse, neglect, or misappropriation. There is no discretion here — these are automatic disqualifiers by law.
⚠️ Confirm the person never worked a client visit. If they somehow were assigned to a client before the background check cleared (which should never happen), this is a licensing violation. File an internal incident report, notify IDPH if required, and document exactly what happened and how it will be prevented in the future.
