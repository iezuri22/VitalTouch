Medicare and Medicaid changes, explained

What changes in 2026 and 2027 and what to do. Last reviewed October 12, 2026.

Medicare open enrollment

You can switch Medicare Advantage plans, or move between Original Medicare and Advantage, from October 15 to December 7, 2026, for 2027 coverage.

More Advantage plans now offer in-home support, often on a prepaid flex card. It is most common in special needs plans and varies by plan and county.

Before you pick a plan, check

  • Whether it lists in-home support services, and how many hours a year
  • Who provides it: the plan, a vendor, or any licensed agency
  • Whether you need a diagnosis, referral or assessment first
  • What the flex card covers, and whether unused money expires

Tell us the plan name and we’ll help you read the benefit. We don’t sell insurance.

By Jan 1, 2027Medicaid

Medicaid work requirements

Under the July 2025 federal budget law (Public Law 119-21), Medicaid expansion adults aged 19 to 64 must show 80 hours a month of work, community service, job training or school. States must start by January 1, 2027, and may start earlier.

Who is exempt

  • People who are medically frail, including people who are blind, living with a disability, or with a serious or complex condition
  • Parents, guardians and family caregivers of a child 13 or younger, or of a person with a disability
  • People who are pregnant or postpartum, and other groups set by the law

Most people 65 and older are not affected.

If you care for a family member with a disability, make sure your Medicaid case shows it. Update it through ABE (abe.illinois.gov).

From Jan 2027Medicaid

Renewals every six months

Expansion adults will be rechecked every six months instead of yearly, and retroactive coverage drops to one month. Most people who lose coverage lose it over missed paperwork.

  • Keep your address, phone and email current with the state
  • Answer every Medicaid letter by its due date
  • Put your renewal month on the calendar
July 2024 to 2032Medicare

Dementia respite through GUIDE

GUIDE is an eight-year Medicare program for people living with dementia and their family caregivers. Medicare pays participating programs up to about $2,500 a year for respite, depending on the year and the person’s needs. The money goes to the program, not the family, and doesn’t roll over.

GUIDE is generally for Original Medicare, not Medicare Advantage. Ask the doctor or memory clinic if they take part.

No changeMedicare

What Original Medicare covers at home

Original Medicare pays for short-term skilled care a doctor orders, usually after a hospital stay, for someone mostly homebound. It does not pay for ongoing help with bathing, dressing, meals, or housekeeping. See the other ways to pay.

VitalTouch doesn’t bill Medicaid yet. If Medicaid is the right route, we’ll point you to an agency that can.
